# Design directions: decision

Decided 16 October 2026, from the three directions in [`brief.md`](brief.md).

## The choice

**Direction B, Opportunities first, with one borrowing from Direction A:** A's three illustrated
program panels, placed on the home page directly after the opportunities. Everything else is
Direction B's, including the opportunity list: a filter sidebar beside rows of facts.

## Why

The directions were judged by one question: which gets a vendor from the home page to an open
opportunity that suits them fastest on a phone, while still explaining the three programs to a
first-time visitor?

- **Reaching an opportunity.** Direction B puts the search and the open opportunities themselves on
  the home page, so a vendor is one tap from an opportunity's details. Directions A and C show no
  opportunities on the home page; a vendor first opens the list, which in A is split by program and
  in C sits below a band of program descriptions.
- **Explaining the programs.** Direction B's compact strip is easy to skip. Direction A's panels give
  each program equal weight, its illustration, what it pays and a way to learn more, so a newcomer
  can compare the three at a glance. Placed after the opportunities, a returning vendor never has
  to pass them.

## What is given up

- Direction A's open spacing and illustrations elsewhere on the site, and Direction C's photographs
  and headline numbers. The award totals stay a compact band.
- A's panels are drawn at Direction B's density, with smaller illustrations, so they sit naturally
  among B's compact sections.

## Rules for extending it to every screen

1. **Opportunities and the way to find them come first** on any screen that lists them.
2. **An opportunity always shows the same facts in the same order:** program and status, title,
   closing date, value, location and Remote OK, then its summary.
3. **Colour marks only an opportunity's status.** Program labels and everything else stay neutral.
4. **The programs are explained by A's panels where they are introduced,** on the home page.
   Elsewhere they appear as the opportunity type filter and as labels.
5. **Dense by default:** small text for facts, the tighter spacing tokens, and one primary action
   per screen.
6. **The only imagery is the original program illustrations,** inside the program panels.
7. **On phones,** rows become stacked cards with the facts on two lines, panels stack at full width,
   and the header and footer are the shared phone versions.
